# Summary

The Microwave Laboratory is a research unit focused on the study and application of microwave technology across diverse
fields. Its activities encompass advanced research and industrial development through national and international
collaborations. Key areas of investigation include microwave imaging for medical applications like brain stroke
detection, environmental monitoring such as snow and glacier analysis using microwave radar systems, and deep-space
communication technologies operating at Ka-band frequencies. The laboratory is equipped with specialized software and
hardware for the analysis of electromagnetic devices, supporting its cutting-edge research initiatives.

# General Description

The Microwave Laboratory is a dedicated research unit specializing in the broad field of microwave technology. Located
within the Department of Electrical, Computer and Biomedical Engineering, the laboratory actively engages in both
fundamental scientific research and advanced industrial development. It fosters numerous national and international
partnerships with academic institutions and industrial entities, aiming to push the boundaries of microwave
applications.

The laboratory's research agenda is diverse, covering critical areas such as medical imaging, environmental monitoring,
and space communication. In medical applications, the Microwave Laboratory explores innovative techniques like microwave
imaging for the early detection and characterization of conditions such as brain stroke. For environmental studies, the
unit develops and deploys microwave radar systems for precise monitoring of snow and glaciers, contributing to
cryosphere research and hazard assessment.

Furthermore, the Microwave Laboratory is involved in the development of advanced communication systems, particularly
focusing on deep-space communication at high frequencies, such as the Ka-band (32 GHz). The laboratory is well-equipped
with commercial software for the analysis and design of electromagnetic devices, alongside modern computing
infrastructure, enabling complex simulations and data processing essential for its research endeavors.
